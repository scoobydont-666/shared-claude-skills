# Sections Playbook

The default modern structure (US tech), reverse-chronological, single column:

```
[Name]                              ← largest text on the page
[Target title or current title]     ← one line, mirrors the role you want
[City, ST · email · phone · LinkedIn/GitHub URL]   ← in the BODY, not the header

SUMMARY            (3–4 lines)
SKILLS             (grouped, keyword-dense, true)
EXPERIENCE         (the spine — reverse-chronological)
PROJECTS           (optional — strong for ICs / career-changers)
EDUCATION          (short once you have experience)
CERTIFICATIONS     (if relevant)
```

Order by what sells *this* candidate for *this* role. Early-career or career-changers
may put Projects/Education above thin Experience. Senior people lead with Experience.

## Header / contact
- Name biggest. Then a **target-title line** ("Senior Platform Engineer") that mirrors
  the posting — this primes both the recruiter and the keyword match.
- One line of contact **in the body**: City, ST · email · phone · LinkedIn (and GitHub /
  portfolio if relevant). No full street address. No photo (US/UK tech). No pronouns line
  unless the user wants it.

## Summary (NOT an objective)
3–4 tight lines: **who you are + level + specialization + 1–2 signature, quantified
wins + the value you bring.** No "seeking a challenging role where I can grow" — that's
about you, not them. Lead with the candidate's positioning.

> [Role and specialization], with [verified experience]. Delivered [documented outcome] through [specific method].

Tailor the specialization clause to the target. Cut every adjective that isn't load-bearing.

## Skills
- **Grouped by category** (e.g., *Cloud & Infra · IaC & Automation · Languages ·
  Observability · Leadership*), not one undifferentiated wall.
- **Keyword-dense and true** — mirror the JD's tools. Plain text, comma- or
  pipe-separated, wrapping across lines (NOT a multi-column table → breaks ATS).
- Put the **most target-relevant** group first. Don't list 60 skills; list the ones the
  role needs plus your genuine differentiators.
- No proficiency bars/graphics (unreadable to ATS, and self-rated dots mean nothing).

## Experience (the spine)
Per role:
```
Company — Title                                   City, ST · MMM YYYY – MMM YYYY
• [strongest quantified accomplishment]
• [next accomplishment]
• ...
```
- **Title and company both visible**; level legible at a glance.
- 4–6 bullets for recent/relevant roles, fewer as they age. See `bullet-craft.md`.
- **Promotions within one company**: stack titles under one company block with sub-dates
  — shows growth and avoids duplicate company headers.
- **Contract/short stints**: label "(Contract)" so gaps/tenure read correctly.
- Optional one-line role scope before bullets for big jobs: *"Owned a $4M platform budget
  and a 12-person org across 3 product lines."*

## Seniority calibration (what each level leads with)
- **Junior / IC (0–5 yrs):** what you built and shipped; concrete tech; projects;
  learning velocity. 1 page.
- **Senior IC (5–12 yrs):** technical impact at scale; cross-team influence; systems you
  own; quantified reliability/perf/cost wins. 1–2 pages.
- **Manager / Senior Manager:** *scope* (team size, budget, surface owned) + *outcomes*
  (delivery, reliability, business impact) + *people* (hiring, growth, retention). Keep
  enough technical depth to stay credible. 2 pages.
- **Director / VP / Exec:** org scale, business/P&L outcomes, strategy, transformation.
  Outcomes over activities. 2 pages (rarely 3).
- **Principal / Staff:** technical leadership, org-wide leverage, the hardest problems
  solved, multipliers on other engineers. 2 pages.

## Education
- Degree, institution, graduation year (omit year if managing age and you have 15+ yrs
  experience). GPA only if recent grad and strong (≥3.5).
- Move **below** Experience once you have a few years of work. Recent grads keep it near
  the top.

## Optional sections (use when they add signal)
- **Projects**: great for ICs, career-changers, open-source, side work with real users.
- **Certifications**: cloud (AWS/Azure/GCP), K8s (CKA/CKAD), security, PM — list with
  year if current.
- **Publications / Talks / Patents**: for research, devrel, principal/staff, academic.
- **Awards / Honors**: brief, only if notable.
- **Leadership / Community**: if relevant to the role's seniority.

## Length rules
- **1 page**: < ~10 yrs experience, or any early/mid-career.
- **2 pages**: senior / 10+ yrs / management — standard and expected.
- **3 pages**: principal/exec/academic only. **US federal resumes are capped at 2 pages** (USAJOBS rule effective 2025-09-27) — never 3.
- A **6-page LinkedIn PDF export is not a resume.** Cut to the spine: drop old roles to
  one line, compress duties into top wins, kill repetition. Length is earned by
  relevance, never by tenure alone.

## Field variants (don't apply US-tech defaults blindly)
- **Academic CV**: long-form, full publication/teaching/grant list, no length limit, no
  "impact bullets" — different genre.
- **US federal resume**: **2-page limit** (USAJOBS rule effective 2025-09-27; agencies
  generally accept up to two pages unless the announcement states an exception). Include
  hours/week, salary, supervisor/contact info, and explicit keyword matching to the
  announcement — but stay within two pages.
- **EU / international CV**: a photo, date of birth, and nationality are sometimes
  expected (varies by country) — opposite of US/UK norms. Confirm the market.
- **Finance / law / consulting**: more conservative formatting, often deal/transaction
  lists, GPA retained longer.

When in doubt, **ask the user the field and market** before applying defaults.
