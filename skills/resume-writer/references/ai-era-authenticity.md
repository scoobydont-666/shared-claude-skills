# AI-Era Authenticity — sound human, not generated

Recruiters and hiring managers now see a flood of LLM-written resumes and have learned
to spot — and distrust — the tells. A resume that reads as machine-generated signals low
effort and can quietly tank a strong candidate. The irony: you can use AI to write a
*better* resume, but only if you strip the generic LLM fingerprint and keep the specific,
checkable, human detail. **Specifics are the antidote to slop.**

## The banned vocabulary (LLM / corporate-cliché tells)
Cut or replace these. They're vague, overused, and scream "generated/templated":

`spearheaded` · `leveraged` / `leverage` · `synergy` / `synergize` · `passionate` ·
`results-driven` · `results-oriented` · `dynamic` · `proven track record` (then prove
it instead) · `seamless` / `seamlessly` · `robust` · `cutting-edge` · `state-of-the-art` ·
`world-class` · `best-in-class` · `game-changer` · `move the needle` (in prose) ·
`think outside the box` · `wear many hats` · `hit the ground running` · `team player` ·
`detail-oriented` (show it) · `go-getter` · `self-starter` · `delve` · `tapestry` ·
`navigate the landscape` · `in today's fast-paced world` · `at the end of the day` ·
`utilize` (→ use) · `facilitate` (often → "ran/led/did") · `orchestrate` (overused).

> Replace adjectives-about-yourself with **evidence**. Don't say "detail-oriented" —
> show the bug you caught that saved the release. Don't say "proven track record" —
> show the track record.

## Structural tells of AI text
- **Uniform rhythm**: every bullet the same length, same cadence, same em-dash in the
  same spot. Vary length and structure.
- **Em-dash overuse**: LLMs love `—`. One per few bullets, max. Vary punctuation.
- **Triads everywhere**: "fast, reliable, and scalable" on every line. Break the pattern.
- **Hedging / fluff connectors**: "in order to", "with a focus on", "responsible for
  ensuring that". Cut to the verb.
- **Generic openers**: "Highly motivated professional with a passion for…" → delete.
- **Vague intensifiers**: "significantly", "substantially", "various", "numerous",
  "several" → replace with the actual number.
- **Smart/curly quotes and fancy glyphs** copied from a chat UI → normalize to plain.

## The authenticity tactics
1. **Anchor every bullet to a checkable specific** — a number, a named system, a real
   tool, a concrete before→after. AI can't fabricate the candidate's actual `820ms →
   140ms`; that specificity is what reads as real.
2. **Keep the candidate's real voice and word choices.** If they say "killed the flaky
   test suite", that's more human than "remediated test instability."
3. **Name real nouns**: actual technologies, team names, products, scales. Specific nouns
   beat abstract adjectives every time.
4. **Vary sentence architecture.** Some bullets lead with the metric, some with the verb,
   some with the scope. Not a template.
5. **Cut anything you can't defend in an interview.** If a line would collapse under "tell
   me more about that," it's slop — fix it or remove it.

## Honesty is non-negotiable (and practical)
- Never invent metrics, titles, dates, employers, or credentials. Beyond the ethics, an
  inflated resume dies in the interview and burns the relationship.
- If a number would help and the user doesn't have it, **ask** — don't generate one.
- "AI-friendly" means *parses well and matches keywords truthfully*, NOT "stuffed with
  keywords" or "embellished by a model." Those backfire with the LLM screener and the
  human alike.

## Quick de-AI pass (run before shipping)
- [ ] Zero words from the banned list (or each remaining one is literally, specifically true)?
- [ ] Bullet lengths and structures vary (not a uniform template)?
- [ ] Em-dashes and triads not overused?
- [ ] Every bullet has a concrete, checkable specific (number / named system / real tool)?
- [ ] No adjective-about-self without evidence behind it?
- [ ] Reads in the candidate's plausible voice, not generic corporate-AI?
